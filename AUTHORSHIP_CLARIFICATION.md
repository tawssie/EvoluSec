# Authorship and research-contribution clarification

## Publication

**NAD+ cleavage activity by animal and plant TIR domains in cell death pathways**  
_Science_ (2019), 365(6455), 793–799  
DOI: [10.1126/science.aax1911](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aax1911)  
PubMed: [PMID 31439792](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31439792/)

The published author list includes both **Jhih-Siang Lai** and **Gabriel Foley** as separate co-authors.

## Correct attribution

Jhih-Siang Lai's University of Queensland doctoral thesis includes this Science publication and explicitly records his contribution to the **"Bioinformatic analysis of TIR domains"** section.

The thesis records the following contributions by Jhih-Siang Lai:

1. writing specifically for the section;
2. data collection and annotation for the bioinformatics analysis;
3. preparation of figures for the section and supplementary information; and
4. scientific concept/design.

Accordingly, **Jhih-Siang Lai is a documented contributor to the bioinformatics / structural-bioinformatics work on TIR domains associated with this publication**.

These specific documented contributions should **not** be reassigned from **Jhih-Siang Lai** to **Gabriel Foley**. Lai and Foley are different researchers and different co-authors.

## Why the thesis record is significant

This contribution statement is part of a UQ doctoral thesis rather than an informal biography or retrospective CV statement.

The University of Queensland's current **Higher Degree by Research Examination Guideline** states that, for each scholarly work included in a thesis, the candidate must make a **substantial contribution** to:

- conception and design of the project or its components;
- analysis and interpretation of the research data; and
- drafting and production of significant parts of the scholarly work.

The guideline also requires a clear statement of authorship and contribution for each scholarly work included in the thesis.

UQ's current **Higher Degree by Research Examination Policy** states that thesis examination is conducted by at least two recognised experts in the discipline who are external to UQ and independent of the conduct of the candidate's research.

Therefore, the thesis contribution statement is appropriately treated as **formal academic evidence of Jhih-Siang Lai's documented contribution**, not merely as an unverified personal claim.

Important distinction: the **Science article is peer reviewed**; a UQ doctoral thesis is more accurately described as **formally examined by independent external disciplinary experts**, rather than as "peer reviewed."
